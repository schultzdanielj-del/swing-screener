# 3-4 Day Bounce (3-4db)

## Pattern Summary

A short setup (or failed long continuation) where price pulls back on the D1 timeframe, then produces a weak 3-4 day bounce on declining volume that stalls at a key level. The entry is a breach and fail of that key level.

## Key Characteristics

1. **D1 Pullback** — Stock has a meaningful pullback from a recent high on the daily chart.

2. **Weak 3-4 Day Bounce** — After the pullback, price bounces for approximately 3-4 days. The bounce is characterized by:
   - Declining volume throughout the bounce (buyers drying up)
   - Small-bodied candles / lack of conviction
   - Price grinds up without real thrust

3. **Bonus: Large ATR Red Candle in Preceding Pullback** — If the pullback includes a high-volume, large-range red daily candle, the setup is stronger. This signals institutional selling / distribution.

4. **Stall at Key Level** — The bounce ideally stalls at a recognizable level:
   - Prior support turned resistance
   - Round number
   - Horizontal level from prior price action
   - Moving average (10, 20, or 50 SMA)

5. **Entry: Breach & Fail** — The trigger is when price breaches the key level intraday but fails to hold above it, closing back below. This confirms the level as resistance and the bounce as exhausted.

## What Makes This Setup Work

The weak bounce on declining volume reveals that the pullback was not just profit-taking — buyers are unable to reclaim momentum. The stall at a key level gives a defined risk point. The breach & fail confirms sellers are in control at that level.

## Visual Markers to Look For

- Rising price over 3-4 candles after a drop, but volume bars shrinking each day
- Price approaching a horizontal line / prior high from below
- One or more wicks poking above the key level then closing below
- MAs starting to flatten or curl down during the bounce

## Notes on Examples

The example charts vary intentionally:
- **Pre-entry vs post-entry** — Some show the setup forming (before the breach & fail), others show the result after entry. Both are useful for pattern recognition.
- **Annotated vs clean** — Some have key levels marked with horizontal lines and labels (e.g., "3db b&f ($20)"), others are unannotated. The vision model should learn to identify the pattern regardless of whether levels are drawn on the chart.
- When matching new charts, focus on the **shape of the pattern** (pullback → weak bounce on declining volume → stall near resistance) rather than relying on annotations being present.
